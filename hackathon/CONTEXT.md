# CONTEXT.md

The vocabulary of the team's world. Use these words rather than generic
equivalents.

> Organiser: fill this in before the session, from a pre-event survey or a
> short call with the team. Keep the headings, replace the guidance under each
> one with real entries, and delete any section that does not apply. Mark a
> term you inferred rather than heard with **[check]** so the agent confirms it
> before relying on it. A filled-in example is in `examples/`.

## The core unit of work

The thing everything is organised around: a show, a matter, a campaign, a
deal, a patient, a shipment, an account. Define it, and the words for its
stages, because "in the cycle" or "closed" means something precise to them.

- **[Term]**: [what it means, and how the team uses the word]

## The people involved

The roles that turn up in their emails and trackers, and how they differ from
each other. Two roles that look the same from outside are often handled by
different people with different data.

- **[Role]**: [who they are, what they owe or are owed, what gets chased]

## The recurring work

The jobs that fill their week: the chasing, the reconciling, the reports, the
content. Name the documents and the trackers involved.

- **[Job or document]**: [what it is, where it lives, who updates it]

## Systems

Which tool holds which data, and which one is the source of truth when two
disagree. Include the everyday ones (email, spreadsheets, chat, shared drives)
as well as the specialist ones.

- **[System]**: [what comes out of it, and in what format]

## Projects currently in flight

Names the agent will meet in file names, subject lines and spreadsheet tabs, so
it recognises them as projects rather than noise. Note any naming convention,
such as year suffixes that make "Project 2026" and "Project 2027" separate
things with separate trackers.

- **[Project]**: [one line]

## How they talk about the problem

Recurring phrases from the survey, and what each one actually means. These are
usually descriptions of the same underlying job in the team's own words.

- "[phrase]": [what it means]
