# CONTEXT.md

The vocabulary of Pico's world. Use these words rather than generic equivalents.

> Drafted from the pre-event survey. Terms marked **[check]** are inferred from
> how people used them and should be corrected before the session.

## The core unit of work

**Show** — a single trade show, conference, exhibition or festival. Everything
is organised around shows. People work several at once, often years apart:
someone might be running a 2026 show and a 2028 one in the same week.

**Show cycle** — the full arc of one show, from early planning through onsite
delivery to post-show reconciliation. "In the show cycle" means the ongoing
run of work, not one deadline.

**Onsite** — the days the show is actually happening, and everything
physically at the venue. Distinct from the months of preparation.

## The people a show involves

**Exhibitor** — a company that buys floor space and puts up a booth. High
volume, transactional, lots of chasing for payment and paperwork.

**Sponsor** — a company that pays for visibility and branding rather than, or
alongside, floor space. Fewer of them, larger deals, longer sales cycle with
budget approvals and compliance sign-off. Not interchangeable with exhibitor.

**Speaker** — someone presenting in the conference programme. The source of
enormous chasing work: bios, headshots, disclosures, presentation titles,
slides, registration, travel.

**Delegate** — a paying conference attendee. **Visitor** — someone attending
the exhibition floor, often free. Different registration paths, different data.

## Speaker and programme work

**Bio** — a speaker's biography, for the programme and website.

**Disclosure** — a speaker's declaration of conflicts of interest or commercial
relationships. Standard requirement at medical and scientific conferences, of
which Pico runs many. **[check]**

**Abstract** — a proposed paper or presentation submitted for review. Gets
accepted or rejected, which triggers notification emails to the author.

**Programme** — the schedule of sessions, speakers and timings. Changes
constantly, and every change has to propagate to the website, the app, printed
materials and the slides folder.

**Speaker tracker** — the spreadsheet holding who has submitted what. The thing
everyone is manually updating from email.

## Exhibition and operations work

**Booth** — an exhibitor's stand on the show floor.

**Fascia** — the name board across the top of a booth. **Fascia name
submission** is the exhibitor telling you what to print on it, and is chased
constantly. **[check]**

**Booth submission** — the package of forms an exhibitor owes before the show:
fascia name, equipment orders, logistics, contractor details. **[check]**

## Marketing

**EDM** — Electronic Direct Mail. A marketing email campaign. "Content
development for EDMs" means writing and building those emails.

**Socials** — social media assets, usually needing design work per platform.

## Money and reporting

**Masterlist** — the authoritative spreadsheet of record, usually registration
and revenue. Manually updated, referenced by everyone. **[check]**

**Reconciling** / **reconciliation** — checking two sources that should agree
and finding where they don't. Registration platform against finance report,
exhibitor payments against orders, revenue against P&L. Comes up more than
almost anything else.

**P&L** — profit and loss statement, per show.

**Registration report** — the breakdown of who has registered, cut by ticket
type, country, and conference session.

## Systems

**Gevme** — the registration and event management platform. Registration data,
ticket types, delegate records, revenue figures come from here.

**Monday.com**, **ClickUp** — project management tools. Different teams use
different ones.

**Brevo** — email marketing platform.

**Tally.so** — form builder, used for collecting submissions.

**SharePoint** — where shared files and lists live. Often the destination for
information manually copied out of email.

**Copilot** — Microsoft's AI assistant, already available to some of the team.

Everything else is standard Microsoft: **Outlook** for email, **Teams** for
chat and calls, **Excel** for the trackers, **PowerPoint** for decks.

## How they talk about the problem

Recurring phrases from the survey, worth recognising as descriptions of the
same underlying job:

- "chasing" — following up repeatedly for something someone owes you
- "blasting" — sending one message to a large list
- "churning" data — processing registration numbers into a report
- "falling through the cracks" — the thing everyone is trying to prevent
- "management-ready" — formatted and narrated for senior leadership, not raw
